    The short version

    What is SEO for restaurants?

    Restaurant SEO is the work of making a restaurant appear when nearby people search for somewhere to eat. It has three parts. Local SEO controls whether you appear in Google Maps and the three-listing local pack. On-page SEO controls whether your website ranks for the dishes, cuisines and neighborhoods you serve. AI search optimization controls whether assistants like ChatGPT and Gemini name you when asked for a recommendation. All three run on the same foundation: accurate listings, readable text, and structured data a machine can trust.

    Most restaurant SEO advice is a decade old. It tells you to stuff your city name into a title tag and buy directory links. That advice was mediocre then and it is actively unhelpful now, because the systems deciding who gets recommended have changed underneath it.

    This page covers what actually moves the needle in 2026, in the order we would do it.

    The three layers

    How restaurant SEO actually works

    Think of it as three separate scoreboards. You can win one and lose the others. Most restaurants are unknowingly playing only the first, and playing it badly.

    1
    Local SEO

    The map pack: proximity, prominence, relevance

    Google decides which three businesses appear in the local pack using three signals. Proximity is how close you are to the searcher, and you cannot change it. Relevance is how well your listing matches what they asked for, which you control through categories, attributes, services and the words on your site. Prominence is how well known and well regarded you are, driven by reviews, citations and the strength of your web presence.

    Because proximity is fixed, the entire game is relevance and prominence. A restaurant four blocks further away will beat you if its categories are precise, its reviews are recent and its menu is readable. That is genuinely good news, because those are the two things you can change.

    Behavioral signals matter more than they used to. Clicks, calls, photo views, direction requests and review cadence all feed local rankings. A profile people actually interact with outranks a profile that merely exists.

    2
    On-page SEO

    Your website: dishes, neighborhoods, occasions

    Your site's job is to cover every way somebody might describe what you sell. That means far more than a homepage that says "Italian restaurant in Denver." It means a readable text menu where every dish is findable, pages for the neighborhoods you draw from, and content for the occasions people search around: date night, private dining, large groups, brunch, patio, late night, gluten free.

    Speed and mobile layout are part of this, not separate from it. 73% of local business searches start on a phone, and a page that takes six seconds to show a menu loses the guest to the restaurant next door regardless of where it ranks.

    3
    AIO / GEO

    AI search: being quotable, not just rankable

    This layer is new and it rewards something slightly different. Classic SEO rewards being rankable. AI visibility rewards being quotable and verifiable. A model assembling a recommendation needs facts it can state with confidence: this restaurant serves this cuisine, in this neighborhood, at this price, open these hours, and multiple independent sources agree.

    Where models pull from varies, but the pattern is consistent: structured listings, review platforms, local directories, food guides, and pages with clean schema markup. Consistency across those sources is what produces confidence, and confidence is what gets you named.

    There is one more reason to care. BrightLocal found that 82% of consumers read AI-generated review summaries, and 23% would decide based on that summary alone. Even people who never open ChatGPT are now reading AI-written descriptions of your restaurant.

    Stop guessing

    The five kinds of searches that bring people to restaurants

    Restaurant keywords are not one pile. They fall into five groups, each with a different intent, a different level of competition, and a different page that should answer it.

    Highest intent

    Dish-level

    "birria tacos near me," "best pad see ew denver," "green chile smothered burrito." The person already knows what they want to eat. They are minutes from a decision and often already in the car. These are the easiest to win and the most valuable, and they are impossible to rank for if your menu is a PDF.

    High intent

    Cuisine plus place

    "italian restaurant rino," "sushi near union station," "mexican food capitol hill." Competitive, but winnable with neighborhood pages and precise Google categories. This is where most restaurants focus, which is exactly why it is crowded.

    High intent

    Occasion and constraint

    "restaurants for large groups denver," "private dining room," "gluten free brunch," "dog friendly patio," "open late near me." Enormously underserved. Almost nobody builds pages for these, and the person searching has a specific need that most competitors cannot meet.

    Immediate

    Near me and open now

    "restaurants near me," "food open now," "places to eat nearby." Driven almost entirely by your Google Business Profile rather than your website: accurate hours, correct categories, proximity and review strength. You win these on the profile, not the page.

    The new one

    Conversational and AI-style queries

    "where should I take my parents for dinner in denver," "somewhere quiet enough to have a conversation," "a good first date restaurant that isn't too expensive," "I want something warm and comforting tonight." These are full sentences with context, constraints and feeling. They are what people type into an AI assistant and what they say out loud to a voice assistant.

    You cannot rank for these the old way, because there is no single keyword to target. You win them by having enough readable, specific, well-structured information that a model can match your restaurant to a nuanced request. A page that says "cozy," "quiet," "good for conversation," "under $30 a head" and "street parking" in plain text is answerable. A page with a hero image and a PDF is not.

            The agentic foundation

            The schema markup every restaurant site should have

            Schema is how you hand a machine facts instead of prose. It is invisible to guests and decisive for AI systems, because a model that can read your hours as data does not have to guess them from a sentence.

            What schema markup does a restaurant website need?

            At minimum: a Restaurant entity with name, address, phone, geo coordinates, price range and cuisine; OpeningHoursSpecification covering regular and holiday hours; a Menu with MenuSection and MenuItem nodes carrying names, descriptions and prices; and FAQPage markup on any page answering common questions. Together these let search engines and AI assistants state facts about you with confidence rather than inferring them.

            Restaurant

            The core entity. Include servesCuisine, priceRange, acceptsReservations, hasMenu, and precise geo coordinates. The cuisine field is what lets a model match you to "Italian" without parsing your homepage copy.

            OpeningHoursSpecification

            Regular hours plus validFrom and validThrough for holiday exceptions. Voice assistants read hours almost exclusively from structured sources, so this is what stands between a guest and a locked door.

            Menu, MenuSection, MenuItem

            The most under-used markup in the industry. Each dish becomes a discrete object with a name, a description, a price and optional dietary properties. This is what makes dish-level search and AI dish recommendations possible.

            FAQPage

            Question and answer pairs marked up explicitly. AI systems extract these readily because the structure removes ambiguity about what is a question and what is the answer. Visible question count must match the markup exactly.

            BreadcrumbList and WebPage

            Tells machines where a page sits in your site and what it is about. Add a speakable property to your WebPage node identifying the passages suitable for voice assistants to read aloud.

            What to leave off

            Do not mark up review ratings you host yourself on your own site — self-serving review markup violates Google's policy and can trigger manual action. State your real rating in visible copy and let the review platforms carry the structured version.

            One rule that trips up almost everyone

            Your schema must describe what a visitor can actually see on the page. Marking up nine FAQs while showing four, or listing menu items you no longer serve, is a structured data violation and it damages the machine confidence you were trying to build. Keep the markup and the page in lockstep, and audit them together whenever the menu changes.

            Escape the proximity trap

            Neighborhood and occasion pages, done without spam

            Proximity is the one local ranking factor you cannot change. Neighborhood pages are how you reach beyond it — but only if you build them the right way.

            The wrong way, which still gets sold

            Copy one page forty times, swap the city name, publish. This was standard agency practice for a decade and it now produces almost nothing. Google identifies near-duplicate templated pages easily, and AI models find no distinguishing information to work with, so they have no reason to name you for any of those areas.

            Worse, it dilutes your site. Forty thin pages compete with each other and with the pages that actually matter.

            The right way

            Build a page only for a neighborhood where you can say something true and specific that nobody else can. Do you get the pre-show crowd from a nearby theater? Is there a particular commute pattern that fills your bar at 5:30? Do you handle parking differently for guests coming from that direction?

            Three genuinely distinct neighborhood pages will outperform thirty templated ones every time, and they will hold up as search systems get better at spotting filler.

            What belongs on one

            • How to get there from that specific area, including parking or transit.
            • What guests from that neighborhood tend to order or come in for.
            • Real landmarks and cross streets, not just the district name.
            • Timing that matters locally: pre-show, post-game, farmers market Saturdays.
            • A distinct question-and-answer block written for that area.

            Occasion pages beat geo pages

            If you only have appetite for a handful of new pages, build occasion pages instead of neighborhood ones. Private dining, large groups, dietary needs, patio, late night. Competition is far lower, intent is far higher, and the person searching has a specific requirement most of your competitors quietly cannot meet.

            Two or more rooms

            SEO for restaurants with multiple locations

            Multi-location restaurants make one mistake more than any other: running every location off a single page with an address list at the bottom. Search engines cannot rank a page for four neighborhoods at once, and AI models cannot confidently state hours when four sets of hours share one URL.

            Each location needs its own page, its own Google Business Profile, its own schema entity with its own geo coordinates and hours, and ideally its own review flow. The shared brand story can live once on an about page and be linked from each.

            The second common error is duplicating content across location pages. If all four say the same thing with a different address, you have built the templated-page problem at smaller scale. Give each one the local specifics only that room has.

            Per-location checklist

            • Its own indexable page at a clean URL.
            • Its own claimed Google Business Profile and Apple listing.
            • Its own schema entity with unique geo coordinates.
            • Its own hours, including holiday exceptions.
            • Its own menu if the menus differ at all.
            • Its own photos, taken in that room.
            • Reviews requested and answered per location.
            Still being sold in 2026

            Five restaurant SEO myths worth unlearning

            Each of these was reasonable advice at some point. None of them is now, and several will actively cost you.

            1

            "You need to post on social media for SEO"

            Social posts are not a direct ranking factor. Social media is genuinely useful for restaurants — video is a rising recommendation channel and people do discover rooms on Instagram — but it belongs in your marketing plan on its own merits, not as an SEO tactic. Posting daily will not move your map pack position. Fixing your primary category will.

            2

            "More keywords on the page means better rankings"

            Stuffing every neighborhood in the metro into your footer stopped working over a decade ago and now reads as a quality problem. Worse, it makes your page harder for an AI model to summarize, because the signal about what you actually are gets buried. Say clearly what you serve and where you are, once, in plain language.

            3

            "You need hundreds of backlinks"

            For a local restaurant, citations and consistency matter far more than link volume. A handful of mentions on genuine local guides, food directories and city publications does more than hundreds of purchased links, which carry real risk. Restaurants compete in a local radius, not a global one.

            4

            "Just get more reviews"

            Volume alone is not the lever people think. Recency and response matter enormously: 74% of consumers only want reviews from the last three months, and 42% are unlikely to use a business that never replies. Forty reviews from 2022 with no owner responses reads worse than fifteen recent ones that are all answered.

            5

            "AI search is a fad, ignore it"

            The usage moved from 6% to 45% in twelve months, and 40% of consumers now say they trust AI platforms for business recommendations. Whether or not it keeps growing at that rate, the work required to be AI-visible is the same work that improves your Google rankings, your voice search results and your guest experience. There is no scenario where doing it wastes your time.

            Spoken, not typed

            Optimizing for voice search

            How do I optimize my restaurant for voice search?

            Answer full questions in plain language on your website, in about forty to fifty words, using the words people actually say out loud. Keep your Google Business Profile hours and phone number exact, because voice assistants read directly from them. Write at an eighth-grade reading level, mark up your pages with structured data, and make sure the answer to each question sits immediately under a heading phrased as that question.

            Voice queries are longer and more conversational than typed ones. Nobody says "italian restaurant denver" to their phone. They say "what's a good Italian place near me that's still open?"

            That difference matters because voice assistants usually return one answer, not ten. Same problem as AI search: there is no second place.

            The practical work overlaps almost entirely with AI optimization. Question-shaped headings, short direct answers, accurate hours, clean schema. Do it once and it serves Google, voice and AI assistants together.

            The agentic layer

            How to get your restaurant cited by AI

            Six steps, in order. This is the work almost nobody in the restaurant industry has started, which is precisely why it is still cheap to win.

            Step 1

            Make your facts machine-readable

            Cuisine, hours, price range, address, phone, menu items and prices as real text with schema markup behind them. A model cannot repeat what it cannot parse. This is the floor — nothing else works without it.

            Step 2

            Make every source agree

            Your name, address, phone and hours must match on Google, Apple Maps, Yelp, Facebook, your website and every directory. Disagreement lowers confidence. A model that is unsure about you simply names someone else.

            Step 3

            Get cited on sources models trust

            Local guides, food directories, city sites and review platforms. AI systems corroborate across sources before naming a business. One mention is an anecdote. Six consistent mentions is a fact.

            Step 4

            Write in answer-first blocks

            Put the question as a heading and the direct answer immediately underneath in forty to fifty words. Models extract passages, not pages. Give them a clean passage to lift.

            Step 5

            Describe the intangibles in plain words

            Quiet. Loud. Good for groups. Patio. Street parking. Kid friendly. Date night. Under thirty a head. These are the constraints inside real conversational queries, and if the words are not on your page you cannot be matched to them.

            Step 6

            Let the crawlers in, then check

            Make sure your robots.txt does not block GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended. Then actually ask the assistants for a recommendation in your category and city, monthly, and log whether you appear.

            The uncomfortable part: you cannot buy your way into an AI answer. There is no ad slot. There is no bid. The only lever is being the restaurant the model has the most consistent, verifiable, well-described information about. That is unusually fair, and it means a twelve-table neighborhood spot can beat a chain if it does the work.

            A 90-day restaurant SEO plan

            You do not need us for most of this. Here is the sequence we would follow, with what to expect at each stage. Steal it.

            1
            Weeks 1–2 · Free

            Fix the foundation

            Claim your Google Business Profile and your Apple Business Connect listing. Set your primary category as precisely as the options allow, then add secondary categories. Enter exact hours including holidays for the next twelve months. Add attributes: patio, takeout, reservations, wheelchair access, parking. Upload fifteen recent photos.

            2
            Weeks 3–5

            Free the menu

            Take the menu out of the PDF and put it on your site as real text, with sections, item names, descriptions and prices. Add menu schema.             What to expect: the largest single gain most restaurants will see, though it takes weeks to index fully.

            3
            Weeks 4–8

            Clean up every listing

            Find every place your restaurant appears and make the details identical. Old phone numbers, prior business names, missing suite numbers, abbreviations that differ. Then add citations on the local guides and food directories that matter in your city.

            What to expect: slow, unglamorous, and one of the most reliable ranking gains available.

            4
            Weeks 6–12

            Build the pages you are missing

            A page for each neighborhood you draw from. Pages for the occasions that fit you: private dining, large groups, brunch, patio, late night, dietary needs. Each one written in answer-first blocks with a question heading and a short direct answer, then depth below.

            What to expect: three to six months to rank competitively, but these pages compound.

            5
            Ongoing from week 1

            Reviews, forever

            Build asking into the service flow rather than leaving it to memory. 78% of consumers were asked for a review last year and 83% of those asked wrote one. Respond to every review within a week, personally and specifically. Never offer anything in exchange.

            What to expect: compounding. Review velocity is the signal that never stops paying.

            6
            Monthly, forever

            Check the AI answers

            Once a month, ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity for the best restaurants in your category and city. Log whether you appear, in what position, and whether the details are right. When something is wrong, trace which source it came from and correct it there.

            What to expect: irregular. Models refresh on their own schedules, so measure the trend across months rather than reacting to any single result.

            Know if it worked

            How to measure restaurant SEO

            Most SEO reports are designed to look impressive rather than to be useful. Here is what to actually watch, and what to ignore no matter how good it looks on a chart.

            Metrics that connect to covers

            • Calls from your Google listing. Available directly in your profile insights. This is the closest thing to a booking you can measure for free.
            • Direction requests. Someone asking for the route to your door is about as high-intent as a signal gets.
            • Menu link clicks. Tracks whether people are getting far enough to consider you seriously.
            • Reservation or order clicks. The end of the funnel, and the number to put at the top of any report.
            • Search terms that surfaced you. Your profile shows the queries that produced views. This tells you which dish and occasion terms you already win.
            • AI mention rate. Manual, monthly, but the only way to track the newest channel. Ask three assistants and log whether you appear.

            Metrics to ignore

            • Impressions. Being shown is not being chosen. Impressions rise for all kinds of reasons unrelated to your work.
            • Keyword rank for a single term. Local rankings vary by the searcher's exact position, so a single reported position is close to meaningless.
            • Domain authority scores. Third-party invented metrics, not used by Google, and largely irrelevant to a local restaurant.
            • Total review count in isolation. Without recency and response rate it tells you very little.
            • Social followers. A fine vanity number. Not connected to whether tables are full on Tuesday.
            • Total sessions. Traffic from the wrong city or the wrong intent is not progress.

            The honest baseline problem

            Restaurants are seasonal, weather-sensitive and event-driven. A 20% jump in August tells you very little on its own, because August is not February. Compare against the same period last year wherever you have the data, and be suspicious of anyone presenting a month-over-month gain as proof their work caused it. Good measurement is patient and admits what it cannot prove.

            Straight answers

            Restaurant SEO questions

            How long does restaurant SEO take to work?

            Google Business Profile corrections can move within days, sometimes hours — accurate hours, precise categories and fresh photos are the fastest-acting changes in local search. A converted text menu typically takes two to six weeks to index fully. Competitive dish and neighborhood rankings take three to six months. AI citation is the least predictable because each model refreshes on its own schedule. Anyone promising a specific ranking by a specific date is guessing.

            How much does restaurant SEO cost?

            It depends almost entirely on what already exists. A restaurant with a claimed profile, a working website and a text menu needs a fraction of what a restaurant starting from zero needs. The honest framing is that the first two weeks of work — claiming listings, fixing categories, setting holiday hours, uploading photos — is free and you can do it yourself today. Paid work should be scoped against what one additional cover per night is worth to you.

            Can I do restaurant SEO myself?

            Yes, and you should do the first two weeks yourself regardless of who you hire. Claiming your Google Business Profile, setting accurate hours, choosing precise categories and uploading current photos require no technical skill and produce the fastest results. The parts that usually need help are converting the menu with proper schema, cleaning up listings at scale, and building out neighborhood and occasion pages.

            Why is my competitor ranking above me with worse food?

            Because rankings measure findability, not quality. They likely have a more precise primary category, more recent reviews, a menu Google can actually read, and consistent details across every listing. None of that requires better cooking. It is genuinely frustrating and it is also entirely fixable, usually within a few weeks of focused work.

            Does my restaurant need a website if I have a Google profile?

            Yes. 54% of consumers visit a business website after reading positive reviews, up from 32% in 2019 — the site is where the decision closes. It is also where your full menu lives as readable text, which is what makes dish-level and AI search possible. A Google profile alone caps how many ways you can be found and gives AI models very little to quote.

            What is the single most important restaurant SEO fix?

            Getting your menu out of a PDF and onto your site as real text. It converts every dish you sell into a searchable phrase, gives AI assistants something specific to quote, and unlocks dish-level searches, which carry the highest intent of any restaurant query. For most restaurants it is one afternoon of work and the largest single gain available.

            Do AI crawlers hurt my site or steal my traffic?

            Blocking them removes any chance of being named in an AI recommendation, which is now the third most popular source of local business recommendations. For a restaurant the calculation is different than for a publisher: you are not selling articles, you are selling tables. Being quoted accurately with your name attached sends people to your door. We recommend restaurants explicitly allow GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended.

            How do I check if ChatGPT knows about my restaurant?

            Ask it directly. Try "what are the best [your cuisine] restaurants in [your city]" and see whether you appear. Then ask "tell me about [your restaurant name]" and check whether the hours, address and cuisine come back correct. Run both across ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, and repeat monthly. If the details are wrong, trace which public source they came from and fix them there.

            Are directory submissions still worth doing?

            Quality ones, yes. Blanket submission to hundreds of low-quality directories, no — that was outdated a decade ago and now mostly creates listings with wrong details that damage your consistency. Focus on the platforms people actually use and the local guides specific to your city. Ten accurate citations beat four hundred sloppy ones.

            Should I put my menu prices online?

            Yes. Hiding prices does not prevent price sensitivity, it just removes you from consideration for anyone filtering by budget — and both search engines and AI assistants use price range to match restaurants to requests like "somewhere good that isn't expensive." Missing prices also means Google has less to work with when building your listing. Publish them and keep them current.
