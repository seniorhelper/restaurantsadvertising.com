The short version

What is restaurant advertising in 2026?

Restaurant advertising is the work of making your restaurant the one that gets named when a hungry person asks where to eat. That used to mean print ads and radio spots. Today it means three things: ranking in Google Search and Google Maps, getting cited by AI assistants like ChatGPT and Gemini, and appearing on the food and travel sites people actually browse. Paid ads can help. But if your restaurant is not findable, paid ads are just rent on attention you never keep.

Here is the part most agencies will not tell you. The way people choose restaurants changed faster in the last twelve months than in the previous ten years. They are still searching. They are just searching somewhere new.

Meanwhile, most restaurants are not set up for any of it. Recent research found that just 35% of small businesses have a Google Business Profile, and only 40% have a dedicated website (BrightLocal SMB Marketing Report, 2025). That is not a marketing gap. That is an open door.

Did you know

90% of restaurants do not fail in the first year

You have heard the number your whole career. Nine out of ten, gone in twelve months. Bankers repeat it. Landlords repeat it. It gets used to talk people out of opening, and to talk owners into panic decisions.

It is not true, and it never was. Researcher H.G. Parsa at Ohio State went looking for the source and reported that after an extensive literature review he could find no evidence of a 90 percent failure rate anywhere. The claim appears to trace back to a television commercial in the early 2000s. No study. No dataset. No peer review.

Parsa's own longitudinal study of 2,439 restaurants found a first-year failure rate of about 26% for independents, 19% in year two and 14% in year three. A separate analysis of Bureau of Labor Statistics data put first-year restaurant failure near 17% — lower than the roughly 19% rate for other service businesses.

We tell you this because we are not going to sell you with fear. Restaurants do not usually die because the food was bad. They die quietly, from not being found, one empty Tuesday at a time.

Where your next hundred guests actually come from

Most restaurant marketing money gets spent on the channel the owner is most comfortable with, not the one guests are using. Here is the real map, channel by channel, with what each one is worth and what it costs you to ignore it.

Where do restaurant customers find you in 2026?

The largest share still starts at Google Search and Google Maps, on a phone. From there the picture has splintered fast. The average consumer now consults six different review sources before choosing a business, AI assistants have become the third most popular recommendation source, and video platforms and Apple Maps are both climbing sharply. There is no single channel to win anymore. There is a set of surfaces you either appear on or you do not.

Google Search and Google Maps

Still the biggest single door, and still where the phone calls and the driving directions come from. But its share is moving. Google's use as a place to read local business reviews fell from 83% to 71% in a single year. That is not collapse — it is dominance loosening. It still deserves your first hour and most of your attention, and 73% of these searches begin on a phone. The practical work here is your Google Business Profile, your categories, your photos, your hours and your review velocity.

AI assistants

The fastest-moving channel in the category and the one almost nobody is working. Usage for local recommendations went from 6% to 45% in twelve months. Unlike a search results page, an AI answer usually names three to five businesses. There is no page two. You are either in the list or you are not mentioned at all, and the person never learns you existed. Being named requires readable text, consistent listings and citations on sources the models trust.

Review platforms, plural

The single biggest change in how people vet a restaurant is that they no longer trust one source. The average consumer now uses six review sites. Facebook, Tripadvisor, Better Business Bureau, Trustpilot and Yellow Pages all gained usage over the past year. Concentrating every review request on Google leaves five other surfaces looking abandoned.

Apple Maps, which almost everyone ignores

Apple Maps nearly doubled in usage in one year, climbing from 14% to 27%. It ranks fourth for where people write reviews, ahead of where it ranks for reading them, because Apple's rating system is a single tap rather than a written review. Most restaurants have never claimed their Apple Business Connect listing. Every iPhone user who asks Siri for directions or searches Maps is being served whatever Apple guessed about you. Claiming it is free and takes about twenty minutes.

Video: YouTube, Instagram, TikTok

Short video is now a recommendation channel, not just a branding one. YouTube, Instagram and TikTok all gained ground as places people go for local business recommendations, and TikTok has launched a Local Explorer program that mirrors Google Local Guides. You do not need a content studio. You need to make the room and the plates worth filming, thank the people who post about you, and ask permission to reuse their clips.

Your own website

The end of nearly every path. 54% of consumers visit a business website after reading positive reviews, up from 32% when that question was last asked in 2019. That is the moment the decision closes. It is also where a large share of restaurants lose the guest, because only about 40% of local businesses have a dedicated site at all, and many of those that do hide the menu in a PDF and the phone number in an image.

What died, and why it matters

One channel fell off a cliff. Local news sites dropped from 48% to 29% as a source of local business recommendations in a single year, driven by newspaper closures and by AI answers absorbing the traffic that used to reach those publishers. If your advertising plan still leans on the local paper, that is the number to look at before you renew.

The lesson underneath all of this is not that Google stopped mattering. It is that a single-channel restaurant is now a fragile restaurant. The places that stay full are the ones showing up consistently across six or eight surfaces, with the same hours, the same phone number and the same menu on every one of them.

From actual audits

Five mistakes we find in almost every restaurant we look at

Not theoretical problems. These are the five things that turn up over and over when we pull a restaurant's visibility apart. Every one of them is free to fix, and every one is costing covers right now.

1

The menu is a PDF

This is the most common and most expensive mistake in the category. A PDF menu, or a photographed menu, is a locked box. Google cannot read the dish names. AI assistants cannot quote your prices or tell someone you serve gluten-free pasta. Every dish you sell — and each one is a phrase somebody types into a search bar — is invisible.

A restaurant with forty items on a text menu has forty chances to be found. The same restaurant with a PDF has zero. Fixing this takes an afternoon and it is usually the single highest-impact change available.

2

Holiday hours were never set

Regular hours get entered once and then forgotten. Then Thanksgiving arrives, or the Fourth of July, or a Monday you always close, and Google confidently tells four families you are open. They drive over. They find a dark room. Some of them leave a one-star review about it, and that review sits on your profile for years.

Special hours can be set a full year ahead in about fifteen minutes. Almost nobody does it. Put it on the calendar for the first week of January and it never bites you again.

3

The photos are three years old

The menu changed. The dining room got redone. The photos did not. Worse, on a lot of profiles the most recent images were uploaded by customers, which means the first impression of your food is somebody's dim phone snapshot of a half-eaten plate.

Fresh photos do two jobs at once: they show people what they are actually walking into, and they signal to Google that the profile is actively managed. Ten good shots a month, taken on a phone in daylight near a window, beats a professional shoot you did once in 2023.

4

Nobody answers the reviews

Not the good ones, not the bad ones. The owner reads them, gets annoyed or pleased, and closes the tab. Meanwhile 89% of consumers expect a response, 80% say they are more likely to use a business that replies to all of its reviews, and 42% are unlikely to use one that never replies.

Speed has also shifted hard. 19% of consumers now expect a response the same day, up from 6% the year before, and 32% want one by the next day. A restaurant with fifty glowing reviews and zero owner responses reads as absent — to the next customer and to Google's ranking systems, which weigh engagement signals.

5

The name, address and phone number disagree across listings

Suite number on one, not the other. Old phone number still live on a directory from 2019. "St" here and "Street" there. A previous owner's business name still attached somewhere. Each one seems trivial. Together they make both Google and every AI model less confident that they know who and where you are.

Confidence is the whole game in local search. A model choosing five restaurants to recommend will quietly skip the one whose details it cannot verify. Consistency is boring, unglamorous work, and it moves rankings more reliably than almost anything else on this list.

Notice what is not on this list

Ad budget. Social media follower counts. A rebrand. A new logo. None of the five most common, most costly problems we find require spending money — they require an afternoon and someone paying attention. That is genuinely good news, and it is also why we would rather tell you the truth than sell you a retainer you may not need.

Plain definitions

Restaurant marketing terms, defined

Agencies use jargon to make simple things sound expensive. Here is what the words actually mean, so nobody can sell you a service you cannot describe.

Cover

One guest served. Not one table, one table turn or one ticket — one person who sat down and ordered. Covers are the unit restaurants actually run on, which is why every number on this site is expressed in them.

Google Business Profile (GBP)

The free listing that controls how your restaurant appears in Google Search and Google Maps: hours, photos, phone, directions, reviews and attributes. Formerly Google My Business. Just 35% of small businesses have one.

Local pack

The block of three business listings with a map that appears near the top of local search results. It captures a large share of clicks and calls for "near me" style searches, which makes those three slots the most valuable real estate in local search.

NAP consistency

Name, address and phone number matching exactly everywhere your restaurant is listed. Mismatches reduce how confident search engines and AI models are about your business, and low confidence means fewer recommendations.

Schema / structured data

Hidden code on a web page that tells machines what things are: this is a restaurant, this is its cuisine, these are its hours, this is a menu item that costs fourteen dollars. It is how a search engine reads a page as facts rather than as text.

AIO and GEO

AI optimization and generative engine optimization. Both describe the work of getting cited inside an AI answer instead of ranked in a list of links. The tactics overlap heavily with SEO, but AI visibility rewards being quotable and verifiable.

First-party ordering

Orders that come through your own website or app rather than a delivery marketplace. You keep the margin and, more importantly, the customer's contact information. Marketplace orders give you neither.

Citation

Any online mention of your business name, address and phone, whether or not it links to you. Directories, food sites and local guides all count. Citations are a major input to local rankings and to which businesses AI models feel safe naming.

Review velocity

How steadily new reviews arrive, as opposed to how many you have in total. It matters because 74% of consumers only want reviews from the last three months. Forty reviews from 2022 read as a restaurant that used to be good.

Dish-level search

Searching for a specific food rather than a category — "birria tacos near me" instead of "Mexican restaurant." These searches carry very high intent and are almost impossible to win if your menu is not readable text.

Restaurant advertising questions

What is restaurant advertising?

Restaurant advertising is the work of getting your restaurant found and chosen by people deciding where to eat. In 2026 that means three channels: Google Search and Google Maps, AI assistants like ChatGPT and Gemini, and the food and city guide sites people browse. Paid ads are one tool inside that, but visibility is the foundation. Without it, ads are rent you pay forever.

How do I get my restaurant to show up on ChatGPT?

AI assistants pull from sources they can read and trust. Start with a complete, accurate Google Business Profile. Put your full menu on your website as real text, not a PDF or an image. Add structured data that identifies your cuisine, hours, location and price range. Then build citations on local directories and food sites the models already crawl. Consistency across every listing matters more than volume — conflicting hours or addresses make a model less likely to name you.

How much does restaurant advertising cost?

It depends on how much is already in place. A restaurant with a claimed Google profile and a working website needs far less than one starting from zero. We scope every engagement against what a single additional cover per night is worth to you, using the same math as the calculator on this page. If we cannot make that math work in your favor, we will tell you rather than take the project.

Do I really need a Google Business Profile?

Yes, and it is still the highest-return free hour in restaurant marketing. Recent research found just 35% of small businesses have one. It is where your hours, phone number, photos, directions and reviews live, and it feeds both Google Maps and the AI tools that increasingly answer "where should I eat." A restaurant without a claimed profile is relying on someone else's guess about its own information.

Are NFC menus better than QR codes?

They solve the same problem more gracefully — a tap instead of a camera, no focusing and no app. But the real difference is what sits behind the tap. Most QR codes open a PDF, which search engines and AI tools cannot read. We build NFC menus as real web pages where every dish is text with structured data, so the menu works for your guests at the table and for the search engines at the same time.

How long does it take to see results?

Google Business Profile fixes can move within days, sometimes hours — corrected hours, new photos and accurate categories are the fastest wins in local search. Organic rankings for competitive dish and neighborhood terms typically take three to six months. AI citation is less predictable because each model refreshes on its own schedule. Anyone promising page one in thirty days is guessing or lying.

Should I stop using DoorDash and Uber Eats?

Not necessarily — but know what they cost. Standard commissions run 15% to 30% per order, and the effective cost often lands between 30% and 40% once promotions, processing and refunds are counted, against a typical independent net margin of 3% to 5%. The sustainable pattern is to use the apps for discovery and then move repeat customers to your own ordering, your own list and your own dining room, where you keep both the margin and the customer's information.

What is the difference between SEO, AIO and GEO?

SEO is search engine optimization — getting ranked in traditional Google results and Maps. AIO and GEO both describe optimizing to be cited by AI systems: appearing inside a ChatGPT recommendation or a Google AI answer rather than in a list of blue links. The underlying work overlaps heavily. Clean structured data, readable content and consistent listings serve all three. The difference is that AI visibility rewards being quotable, while classic SEO rewards being rankable.

How do I get more Google reviews?

Ask. Research shows 78% of consumers were asked to leave a review in the past year, and 83% of those asked went on to write one. Ask at the moment the guest says the meal was good, make it one tap from the check or a table card, and never offer anything in exchange — incentivized reviews violate platform rules and now carry legal risk in the US. Then reply to every review, briefly and specifically.

Yes. We are based in Denver and our Colorado network runs deepest here, but the search and AI visibility work is location independent and we take restaurants nationwide. Local network placement on our Colorado properties naturally applies to Colorado restaurants, while Google, AI search, menu and website work applies anywhere.
